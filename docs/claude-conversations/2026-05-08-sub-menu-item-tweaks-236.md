Good evening! Let me work through these one by one.
Transport in Living — makes sense. Getting around is fundamental to daily life in the Solent, especially given the geography (ferries, fragmented public transport between Portsmouth and Southampton). Transport in Sectors is about the industry; Transport in Living is about "how do I get to work / school / the hospital". Different lens, same topic — exactly your triple helix model working as intended.
Health & Fitness in Living — good combination. They're naturally paired in people's minds and in practice. Mental Health staying separate is the right call — it's distinct enough in intent (someone looking for mental health support isn't browsing gym classes) and as you say, it's a strong issue that deserves visibility.
Work vs Jobs boards — I'd keep Work in Living and drop Jobs boards from Explore. Work covers employment, career development, work-life balance, remote working — all Living lens topics. Jobs boards is essentially a link aggregator, which could easily be a section within the Work landing page rather than its own top-level menu item. That takes Explore to 9, and a 3×3 grid is clean.
Economic Growth in Sectors — absolutely earns its place. The organisations you mention (Solent Growth Partnership, Business South, Southern Creative Catalyst, Hampshire Prosperity Partnership) represent a genuine ecosystem that your audience would want to discover. It's also editorially important — if a core thrust of the project is pointing people toward regional economic development, it should be navigable from the menu, not buried inside another category.
Public & Non-profit — I'd leave them separate while you have the slot. Public Sector and Non-profit serve different audiences with different needs. Someone looking for council services isn't looking for charities, and vice versa. If you add Economic Growth and fill the 36th slot, you still have a clean 6×6. If you ever need to free a slot in future, that's the merge to make — but no reason to do it pre-emptively.
So the net changes would be:

Living: add Transport, merge Health + Fitness → Health & Fitness, keep everything else = 10 items (5×2 restored)
Explore: drop Jobs boards = 9 items (3×3)
Sectors: add Economic Growth = 36 items (6×6)

That's tidy across the board.

That tension is real, but I think you've already answered it yourself — lifestyle choices promote health. The NHS itself constantly pushes that message: exercise as prevention, fitness as treatment for chronic conditions, social prescribing of activity groups. Separating them is arguably more artificial than combining them, because it implies health is only what happens when something goes wrong.
The landing page structure can do the heavy lifting here. A Health & Fitness page with serious health resources at the top (NHS services, GP access, screening programmes) and fitness/activity content below naturally communicates that both belong under the same umbrella without trivialising either. The ordering signals priority.
And Mental Health still standing alone reinforces that you take the serious side seriously. Someone scanning the Living menu sees "Health & Fitness" and "Mental Health" as separate items — that's a clear signal that mental health isn't just lumped in with everything else.
If it still feels uncomfortable after launch, you can always split them back out. That's a menu change and a taxonomy adjustment, not a rebuild. But I suspect once content starts filling the page, the pairing will feel natural.
